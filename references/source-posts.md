## 1. The 1-1-1 Strategy thread — Jan 9, 2022
https://x.com/BowTiedSalesGuy/status/1480223249262944256

> My cold email strategies are quite simple. They're provocative, informal, and most importantly, get results. This one is called the "1-1-1 strategy"

**"What NOT to do" slide (verbatim email):**
> Subject line: Reduce [Prospects Company] Opex by 977,000 Annually
>
> Hi John,
>
> Many of our clients are not just surviving but thriving & growing their bottomline while minimizing their financial exposure through our [link to solution]
>
> A similar client to [your company] reduced their opex by over $900k with us!
>
> I thought you might be open to hearing some ideas and strategies that companies similar to you are doing.
>
> They say the average attention span is 18 minutes.. so how about this:
>
> We set a meeting for 20 min and if you don't like what you hear, I can give you back the 2 minutes? Deal?
>
> Here's my availability:
> 1/15 8am, 9am, 1pm
> 1/16 10am, 11am, 1pm, 4pm
>
> Just send over a time that works and we'll make it happen!

Critique (his bullets): multiple ideas = overwhelming; too much effort = low status ("what C level would send this?"); long email; multiple actions requested (make prospect answer AND make prospect work to choose a time).

**"What TO do" slide (verbatim email + his annotations):**
> Subject line: [Chad's company] x [Prospects first name only]
>
> Hi John,
>
> We just helped a company in your space (unfortunately can't say which one) reduce their OPEX by $977k.
>
> perhaps we should explore some opportunities

Annotations: subject creates status gap ("outnumbered" — company vs individual); "in your space" triggers emotion ("a threat is in YOUR space"); "(unfortunately can't say which one)" = intriguing, "who is it?"; lower-case "perhaps" = high-status IDGAF; vague suggestion inviting them rather than being too suggestive; "some opportunities" = appeals to inner opportunist without overselling.

## 2. Three Foolproof Strategies thread — Apr 11, 2022
https://x.com/BowTiedSalesGuy/status/1513574826425860101

Teaser: "Gm i'm dropping a thread later on 3 strategies for Chad cold emails Includes: - a new cold email template + analysis - example of how to condense your terrible company email templates - how to use 'the cringe filter' as your north star."

- **Strategy #1 — Begin with the End:** "You first start with the decision you want them to make after reading your email. Really focus here on that ONE decision you want them to make. After thats done you then reinforce the ask with the supporting points."

Example email (verbatim, from slide):
> Subject line: [your company name]
>
> John, can you take a look here and let me know if we should jump on a call?
>
> With COVID we're supporting many financial execs like yourself who are struggling with:
>
> -managing the financial impact on the company if someone gets sick
>
> -pressure to be accountable despite a lack of risk and compliance data
>
> look forward to hearing back -

His analysis (verbatim annotations): "Vague & intriguing. 'What do they want with me?' thinks the exec" · "'take a look here' is visual & direct command. better than saying 'take a minute'... nobody likes to be told to spend time!" · "'jump on a call' is visual and implies 'low commitment' & brief without being low status" · "people don't like to be 'helped'.. supporting implies partnership" · pain bullets = "EMOTIONAL PAIN" · "Let me know -" is "more casual + high status than 'Waiting to hear back'.. the '-' visually implies a cliffhanger."

- **Strategy #2 — the cringe filter:** "It's as simple as really focusing on how you feel as you read your message. If there is even the slightest feeling of cringe then you need to revise the message. Rinse & Repeat."

- **Strategy #3 — ruthlessly cut fluff:** "You are going to take your terrible company email templates and practice condensing them while retaining the message. Use the least amount of words (fluff) as possible."

🤡 company template (verbatim, before):
> Hi John,
>
> Just saw the news about your recent round of funding. Congrats! Very impressive!
>
> Usually when a startup raises money, finding the top talent becomes a priority. So, I thought you might be interested in finding out how we helped [competitor] reach their headcount goals.
>
> Our platform allows you to seamlessly manage the entire hiring process. From recruiting the top candidates to onboarding them and keeping employee satisfaction, our platform has got you covered!
>
> I know things at your company must be crazy right now, but if you'd like to learn more, let's set up a quick call.

🐲 condensed version (verbatim, after):
> Hi John,
>
> congrats on the Series A round.
>
> In my experience, startups at this stage have their attention on recruiting the top talent and reaching headcount goals.
>
> We've been providing unique solutions for companies like [competitor 1], [competitor 2], and [competitor 3]
>
> I don't suppose you'd have a few min to jump on a call and see if we can do the same for you?

"This is a pure example of cutting through the noise for a more potent message."

## 6. "6 Lessons from my last consult" — Jul 31, 2025
https://x.com/BowTiedSalesGuy/status/1950936943061049811 (full text quoted in SKILL.md §5; key 🐲/🤡 pairs):
> "1) on Linkedin you see the prospect doesn't have a profile pic. Not very active. 20+ years at the company? They are the top dog. Adjust accordingly."
> "2) Always prospect around why and how a company buys. Deal qualification is basically Timing and Intent with Status quo being your biggest competitor… Stupid copy paste templates are killing your CVR. Timing and Intent > Messaging. This is why two reps using the exact same messaging can get totally different outcomes."
> "3) Don't insult their current setup… 🤡: Are you experiencing these pain points? [inserts numbered list] / 🐲: Even with a strong setup, HR leaders often explore additional support to meet their growth goals."
> "4) Mind your word choice — subject lines like 'Quick question' and 'mind taking a quick look?' trains people not to take you seriously. Nobody gives time or importance to something labeled 'quick.' Speak like what you're saying matters. You are worth their time."
> "5) Flip every feature into its underlying pain because that's how buyers think: 'Create better experiences' ➡️ Poor experiences / 'Improve strategy' ➡️ Current strategy isn't working / 'Increase engagement' ➡️ Employees are not engaged / 'Attract and retain talent' ➡️ Hard to find and keep talent. Best reps sell with both the carrot and the stick."
> "6) Show, don't tell… 🐲: you've clearly built a strong recruiting pipeline that's worked well for mid-level roles.. where I typically see challenges is when companies hit a certain headcount and those same tactics stop working with senior hires.. has that started to show up yet?"
