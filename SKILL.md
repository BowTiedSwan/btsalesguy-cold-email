# BowTiedSalesGuy Cold Email Framework

## Overview

Cold outreach is not a pitch — it is a short, casual note from a high-status peer. The goal of every email is a single, low-friction action (almost always: reply). Everything else — feature lists, calendar links, value props, politeness scaffolding — is noise that marks you as a low-status salesperson (🤡) instead of a busy executive (🐲).

This skill encodes @BowTiedSalesGuy's system as compiled from his X threads (Jan 2022 – 2026), Substack announcements, and consult recaps. Verbatim source material lives in `references/source-posts.md`; copy-paste-ready templates live in `templates/chad-vs-clown.md`.

Core chain of value: **Research → one decision → conversational short draft → intrigue (not cute) → show don't tell → cringe filter → read-aloud test → send, 1–3 touches max.**

## When to Use

- Drafting a cold email to a prospect, exec, or founder
- Rewriting an email that "feels like every other sales email"
- Writing LinkedIn DMs or connection notes
- Following up on a ghosted thread or no-show
- Auditing a sequence before it ships (use the Verification Checklist as a gate)

**Don't use for:** warm intros (leverage the referrer's frame instead), marketing newsletters, legal/compliance mail, or any message where the required action is genuinely complex — those need different structures.

## The Frame (read first, always)

Every principle below serves one frame: **a busy, high-status person is sending a short note to another busy, high-status person.** The buyer should feel they discovered the relevance themselves. You are not chasing; you are filtering.

- **Timing + Intent > Messaging.** Two reps with identical templates get different results; the one who researched wins. Deal qualification is Timing and Intent, with Status Quo as the biggest competitor.
- **Energy and intention leak.** "If any part of you is hoping the buyer doesn't pick up or reply, you will make manifest that outcome. The worst script delivered with positive intentions will outperform the best script with unconscious negative intentions." (Nov 2024)
- **Delivery & timing > script** (cold calls); **intrigue & timing** (cold email). (Dec 2024)

## Step-by-Step Workflow

### 1. Research before writing
Look for public signals: LinkedIn activity, tenure, company changes, funding news, how they currently buy. Know which signals are public (LinkedIn, ZoomInfo) and which are not; combine both in the message. Profile check: no pic + inactive + 20+ years at the company = "top dog" → adjust accordingly (more deference, never insult what they built).

### 2. Begin with the End
Decide the ONE decision you want after reading — almost always "reply so we can see if a short call makes sense." Start from that ask, then add 1–2 supporting points. Never two asks.

### 3. Draft to the 1-1-1 spec
- **One idea only.** Multiple ideas = overwhelming = 🤡.
- **One action only** — and make it "reply," not "pick a time from this list." Making the prospect work = low status.
- **Readable in seconds.** A C-level will not read long emails.
- **Language you'd say out loud.** Lower-case "perhaps," trailing dashes ("look forward to hearing back -"), and slight vagueness ("some opportunities") signal a high-status executive who doesn't try hard.

### 4. Subject line: intrigue or status gap, never a pitch
- 🐲: `[Your Company] x [First name]`, `[prospect's company name]`, `an idea`, `?`, `relevant?`, `via [high-status referral name]`, `a bit confused`, `quick yes/no`.
- 🤡: "Quick question", "mind taking a quick look?", "Just following up" — minimizers train people not to take you seriously. "Nobody gives time or importance to something labeled 'quick.'"
- **Don't take ChatGPT subject lines at face value** — "ask ChatGPT for 10 subject lines, then use none of them." A minor imperfection reads as human; a perfectly polished line reads as spam.

### 5. Body: show, don't tell; flip pain, don't list features
- Instead of "we help companies create better experiences," imply the current reality: "where I typically see challenges is when companies hit a certain headcount and those same tactics stop working… has that started to show up yet?"
- **Flip every feature into its underlying pain** (buyers think in pain): "Create better experiences" → poor experiences. "Improve strategy" → current strategy isn't working. "Increase engagement" → employees are not engaged. Sell with carrot AND stick.
- **Never insult their current setup.** If they built it, direct pain-probing is "calling their baby ugly." 🤡: "Are you experiencing these pain points? [numbered list]" 🐲: "Even with a strong setup, HR leaders often explore additional support to meet their growth goals."

### 6. The Cringe Filter + Read-Aloud test (mandatory gate)
- **Cringe filter:** read the message and focus on how you *feel*. Slightest cringe → revise. Rinse & repeat.
- **Read-aloud test:** read it out loud imagining you're talking to them in person. If it sounds different from how you'd actually speak, edit until relaxed and informal.
- **Cut fluff ruthlessly:** take your company template and condense it while retaining the message — least words possible. See `templates/chad-vs-clown.md` for the canonical before/after.

### 7. Volume & follow-ups: 1–3 touches max
"If your first 1–2 MAYBE 3 cold emails didn't land, your 6th one won't." Long needy sequences look automated ("Hello Paul w,") and torch your status. Cadence:
1. First email.
2. ~2 days later: reply to the thread with `[name?]` — the two best follow-ups are `[name]?` and the Kermit meme.
3. ~2–3 days later: one more nudge, then stop.
- Ghosted after a meeting: one-line "you disappeared" — or put it in the subject line with an EMPTY body for maximum ZFG energy.
- Ghosted on time slots: 🤡 "did you have a chance to book a time?" → 🐲 "John - did you still want to meet?"
- No-show: leave the Zoom after 5 min, decline the calendar invite, then: "Hi John, I was waiting for you in the Zoom but decided to jump into another meeting. If you're still interested you can book a new time here [link]. If I don't hear back I'll assume you've found a vendor and will close out your file."

## LinkedIn-Specific Adjustments

- Same rules as email: short, researched, one idea. **Never mention your company name in a LinkedIn DM** (NGMI).
- 🐲 DM shape: "If you guys are seeing latency issues with new feature rollouts and want a second opinion LMK." Direct & concise.
- Stand out: find a recent post with positive emotional context, reference it, tie your value prop to that context.
- Spammy connect-request notes are 🤡; a generic note is worse than none. Top-dog/inactive profiles need deference, not direct pain language.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Multiple ideas or multiple asks** — the #1 🤡 tell. One idea, one reply.
2. **Making the prospect work** — embedding time-slot menus, "pick a time that works," numbered pain lists to answer.
3. **Minimizer subject lines** ("Quick question") and apologetic openers ("I know you're busy, but…") — low status.
4. **Telling instead of showing** — "I'd love to show you how…" vs. demonstrating insight into their specific situation.
5. **Insulting what they built** — direct pain questions to ego-invested prospects.
6. **Over-polish** — perfect grammar + marketing speak = template = spam. Slight imperfection reads human.
7. **6-email sequences** — volume can't save a bad frame; it just automates the damage.
8. **AI-template smell** — ChatGPT-flavored lines ("I hope this message finds you well") at face value.
9. **Writing before deciding the one decision** — produces meandering drafts that need 3 rewrites.
10. **Negative intention** — if part of you hopes they don't reply, they won't. Fix state before script.

## Verification Checklist (gate before any send)

- [ ] Research done: public + non-public signals identified; top-dog profile adjusted for
- [ ] One decision defined; email starts from that ask (Begin with the End)
- [ ] One idea, one action (reply), readable in <10 seconds
- [ ] Subject creates intrigue/status gap; no "Quick question"; not ChatGPT-polished
- [ ] Features flipped to pain; show-don't-tell; current setup not insulted
- [ ] Cringe filter passed (zero internal wince)
- [ ] Read-aloud test passed (sounds like in-person speech)
- [ ] Fluff cut ruthlessly vs. the company-template version
- [ ] Touch count ≤3 in the sequence; follow-ups are `[name]?` / "you disappeared" tier
- [ ] Zero hope-they-don't-reply energy — positive intention confirmed
